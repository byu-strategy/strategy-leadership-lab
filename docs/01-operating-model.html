Strategy Program Operating Model

The Strategy Program administers four career clubs that serve students across campus. Club membership is open by career interest, not by major: a student headed for consulting, product, or corporate strategy belongs in the relevant club whether they are a strategy major or not. Roughly half the leaders in this room are majoring in something else.

The program has grown by 50% over the last five years and now serves 120 new students per year through the major, and the clubs reach a wider group than that. The operating model below aligns faculty, student leaders, career services, and academic advisement at that scale. It has three parts.

1. Integrated Leadership

Four roles share oversight of the student leadership team. They oversee the clubs on behalf of the whole student population the clubs serve, not only strategy majors.

Role Who Responsibility
Program Director Scott Murff Program strategy and cross-unit coordination
Business Career Director Soraya Cullimore Employer relationships; supervises the three career club co-president pairs
Advisement Director Lisa Giguere Academic advising and graduation planning
Club faculty advisors See below Mentorship, industry connections, domain expertise

2. The Clubs

Each career club exists to open doors and prepare students for a specific profession. The clubs are organized around where students are headed, not what they are majoring in, and are open to students from any major. The Strategy Program administers them and supplies the faculty advisors and career services support.

MCA, PMA, and CSA co-presidents report operationally to Soraya Cullimore, with a dotted line to their faculty advisor. WSA is an affinity group providing community and mentorship; its president reports directly to Lena Lizunova.

Club Full name Mission Faculty advisor
MCA Management Consulting Association Place students at consulting firms, any major Dave Bryce
PMA Product Management Association Place students in PM and product adjacent roles, any major Scott Murff
CSA Corporate Strategy Association Place students in corporate strategy and ops roles, any major Ben Lewis
WSA Women in Strategy Association Community and mentorship for women across majors Lena Lizunova

Ben Lewis is the current CSA advisor. The operating model PDF, written earlier, lists Mark Hansen.

3. Leadership Selection

Leadership positions are open to students from any major. What qualifies someone is credibility in the career track the club serves, not enrollment in the strategy major.

Current co-presidents nominate successors. The hiring committee considers those nominations and makes the final decision. Volunteer VPs are selected by co-presidents with no committee approval required.

Position Count Hiring committee
Presidency: co-presidents, VP Events, VP Marketing 4 Scott Murff
MCA co-presidents 2 Soraya Cullimore and Dave Bryce
PMA co-presidents 2 Soraya Cullimore and Scott Murff
CSA co-presidents 2 Soraya Cullimore and Ben Lewis
VP Alumni 1 Soraya Cullimore and Scott Murff
VP Student Development 1 Lisa Giguere and Scott Murff
WSA president 1 Lena Lizunova

Timeline. Most positions are filled in March and active by May. MCA is the exception: co-presidents are selected in November, after consulting offers settle. Off-cycle graduations can create mid-year vacancies, which the relevant hiring committee fills as needed.

MCA co-president criteria: demonstrated success in consulting recruiting, leadership ability, and credibility to represent the program to top firms.

Employee and Volunteer Roles

Thirteen student leaders are employees; roughly sixteen are volunteers. The difference is scope and time commitment.

Employee Volunteer
Hours per week 8 to 10 3 to 5
Commitment Full year Semester or year
Scope Owns outcomes Owns specific tasks and initiatives
Accountability Performance-based, like any job Lower stakes, can step back when busy
Selection Nominated and interviewed Selected by co-presidents
